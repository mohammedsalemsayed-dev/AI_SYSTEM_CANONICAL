Local AI Development & Research Workstation — Project Specification
1. Goal

Build a hybrid local/cloud AI workstation that acts as a knowledgeable technical partner rather than a simple chatbot.

The AI should be capable of helping with serious, senior-level software projects, game development, research, professional documents, and presentations.

The key philosophy is:

The AI should not blindly agree with me or blindly implement what I ask. It should understand what I'm trying to accomplish, identify things I may have missed, challenge bad decisions, explain its reasoning, and recommend better approaches when appropriate.

The system should feel more like working alongside a senior engineer/researcher/designer than using autocomplete.

4. AI Personality / Behavior

This is one of the most important requirements.

The AI should not behave like an obedient assistant.

It should behave like a knowledgeable peer who is potentially more experienced than the user.

For example:

Bad behavior

User:

"I'm going to put the entire inventory system inside the Player class."

AI:

"Sure! Here's the code."

Desired behavior

AI:

"I'd stop before implementing that. It will work initially, but you're coupling inventory state to the Player actor. That becomes problematic when you introduce NPC inventories, multiplayer replication, persistence, or trading. I'd separate the inventory domain from the player and let the player own/reference it."

The AI should actively identify:

Hidden assumptions
Edge cases
Architectural problems
Security problems
Performance problems
Scalability problems
Maintainability issues
Bad abstractions
Unnecessary complexity
Technical debt
Incorrect premises
Missing requirements

It should be comfortable saying:

"I don't think that's a good approach."

It should also explain why rather than simply rejecting an idea.

19. Senior Reviewer

A separate review stage should be available.

Example:

User idea
   ↓
AI implementation / proposal
   ↓
Senior Reviewer
   ↓
Critique
   ↓
Improved proposal
   ↓
Implementation


The reviewer should specifically look for things the original AI/user missed.

Prompt philosophy:

Assume the developer is competent but may have overlooked non-obvious problems. Identify hidden assumptions, edge cases, scalability problems, security issues, performance problems, architectural flaws, and maintainability issues. Do not blindly agree with the proposed solution.

This is one of the most important features of the entire system.

22. Hardware Strategy

Current hardware:

RTX 5060 8 GB + 24 GB DDR5

The system should prioritize models that fit efficiently into VRAM.

General strategy:

Small/medium local model
        ↓
Mostly GPU
        ↓
Fast everyday operation


Larger models can potentially use:

GPU VRAM
+
System RAM


but CPU/RAM offloading can significantly reduce performance.

Therefore:

Don't automatically choose the biggest model that can technically run.

A fast, capable model with good tooling can be much more useful than a much larger model running painfully slowly.

26. Final Vision

The finished system should allow interactions like:

You:
"I want to build an Android research application that indexes academic PDFs and lets users search them semantically."

The AI:

"Before we choose the framework, there are three requirements that will affect the architecture: offline indexing, document size, and whether embeddings are generated on-device. Here's what I'd recommend..."

Or:

You:
"Here's my Unreal multiplayer inventory architecture."

AI:

"I don't recommend implementing it this way. The architecture works for single-player, but you've introduced three problems with replication and state authority..."

Or:

You:
"Turn these 12 research papers into a professional 15-slide presentation."

AI:

Researches → extracts evidence → compares sources → builds narrative → creates charts → designs slides → generates PPTX → reviews the presentation.

The goal is therefore not:

"I have a local ChatGPT."

The goal is:

"I have an AI development and research environment that works alongside me, understands my projects, has access to my tools and knowledge base, challenges my decisions, creates professional deliverables, and escalates difficult problems to stronger cloud models when necessary."
